# Profession Blueprint Guide

## Purpose

The Profession Blueprint is the foundation of the XcelerateAI Curriculum System.

Its purpose is to develop a complete understanding of a profession before curriculum design begins.

Every curriculum generated by XcelerateAI depends entirely on the quality of this blueprint.

A weak blueprint produces a weak curriculum.
A complete blueprint enables the Curriculum Generator to design accurate learning paths without making assumptions.

The objective is not merely to collect information, but to build a complete mental model of the profession, including the knowledge, skills, tools, workflows, expectations, and standards required to reach professional mastery.

---

# Inputs

Required:

- Profession Name

Optional:

- Specialization
- Target Audience
- Career Goal
- Region or Industry Focus

---

# Output

Generate exactly one document.

ProfessionBlueprint.docx

This document becomes the official knowledge foundation for every subsequent stage of curriculum generation.

---

# Research Principles

The AI must:

- Develop a complete understanding of the profession before thinking about teaching it.
- Research deeply instead of producing summaries.
- Prefer official documentation and industry-recognized sources.
- Resolve conflicting information using industry consensus.
- Distinguish facts from opinions.
- Prioritize current industry practices.
- Never invent information.
- Never omit important competencies because they appear "advanced."
- Think like an experienced professional explaining the profession to another experienced professional.

---

# Thinking Framework

The AI should answer the following questions during research.

## Identity

- What defines this profession?
- Why does it exist?
- What problems does it solve?

## Industry

- Which industries use this profession?
- How important is it today?
- How is it evolving?

## Daily Work

- What does a professional actually do each day?
- What responsibilities are common?

## Knowledge

- What concepts must professionals understand?
- Which academic foundations are required?

## Practical Skills

- What must professionals be capable of building, creating, fixing, or operating?

## Tools

- Which tools are considered industry standards?
- Which technologies are commonly used?

## Collaboration

- Which other professionals do they work with?
- How do they collaborate?

## Career Progression

- Beginner expectations
- Intermediate expectations
- Senior expectations
- Expert expectations

## Mastery

- What distinguishes an expert from a beginner?
- How is competence measured?

## Future

- What trends are shaping this profession?
- Which skills are becoming more important?

# Quality Standards

The blueprint must:

- Be comprehensive rather than brief.
- Be logically structured.
- Avoid repetition.
- Present information objectively.
- Use current industry practices.
- Explain dependencies between skills.
- Clearly separate required knowledge from optional knowledge.
- Identify which competencies are foundational and which are advanced.

---

# Master Prompt

Your task is to become an expert on the requested profession before curriculum generation begins.

Develop a complete mental model of the profession by researching its purpose, responsibilities, workflows, required knowledge, practical skills, tools, technologies, career progression, industry expectations, and standards of mastery.

Do not simply collect facts.

Instead, synthesize the profession into a structured document that accurately represents what someone must know and be capable of doing in order to become a competent professional.

Use official documentation, reputable educational resources, and industry-recognized references whenever possible.

Resolve conflicting information using industry consensus.

Never invent information.

Never omit important competencies simply because they appear advanced.

The final output must be sufficiently comprehensive that another AI can generate a complete professional curriculum using only this document.

# Common Failure Modes

Avoid the following:

- Treating the profession as a list of tools.
- Focusing only on technologies instead of competencies.
- Ignoring practical workflows.
- Ignoring soft skills.
- Producing shallow summaries.
- Repeating information unnecessarily.
- Assuming certifications equal competence.
- Assuming one learning path fits everyone.
- Confusing job descriptions with professional mastery.
- Omitting advanced concepts that professionals are expected to understand.
